What this is

This is the "Go Phish" assignment, an educational phishing-awareness demonstration built for a Georgia Tech course (PUBP 6725). Its purpose is to show how convincingly a real login page can be cloned, and to help people recognize and resist these attacks in the real world.

Red flags you could have caught

  • Lookalike domain: the address wasn't the organization's real one. Always check the URL before typing a password.
  • Manufactured urgency: "verify by [deadline] to stay active" pressures you to act before thinking.
  • Unexpected request: an out-of-the-blue email asking you to log in or "re-verify" is a classic lure.
  • Trust in appearances: a perfect logo, real colors, and a clean layout are easy to copy and prove nothing.

What to do in real life

  • Don't click links in unexpected messages; navigate to the site directly, yourself.
  • Check the domain carefully before entering credentials.
  • Use a password manager (it won't autofill on a fake domain) and enable multi-factor authentication.
  • Report suspicious messages to your IT/security team.
